# Active Listening & Communication Styles

## 1. Active Listening

**Question 1: What are the steps/strategies to do Active Listening?**

1. Do not get lost in your own thoughts while listening.
2. Do not interrupt the person in between — let them finish before you speak.
3. Show body language that indicates you are listening.
4. Make notes if it's an important conversation.
5. Be on the same page by reflecting back what you understood from the conversation and the feelings behind it (Reflective listening).
6. Use door openers (e.g., "That sounds interesting").

---

## 2. Reflective Listening

**According to Fisher's model, what are the key points of Reflective Listening?**

- Listen carefully – Pay attention to what the person is saying.
- Understand the message – Try to understand what the person really means.
- Repeat in your own words – Tell the person what you understood in simple words.
- Understand their feelings – Pay attention to how the person is feeling.
- Ask questions if you are confused – If you don't understand something, ask the person to explain it.
- Do not interrupt – Let the person finish speaking before you reply.
- Avoid assumptions – Don't guess what the person means. Listen and understand first.
- Take notes when needed – In meetings or technical discussions, write down important points.
- Make sure everyone agrees – Confirm that everyone has understood the discussion in the same way.

## 3. Reflection

**Question 3: What are the obstacles in your listening process?**

1. While listening, I sometimes get lost in my own thoughts in the middle of a conversation — this happens to me a lot.
2. I am not interested in the conversation.

**Question 4: What can you do to improve your listening?**

1. Remove all distractions — phone, notifications.
2. Stay fully active in the conversation and keep my focus only on it.

**Question 5: When do you switch to a Passive communication style in your day-to-day life?**

1. When I am having a conversation with my senior.
2. When I meet a person I don't know.
3. When the other person is more needy than me.

**Question 6: When do you switch into an Aggressive communication style in your day-to-day life?**

1. When I argue with a person who is saying something wrong.
2. When someone blames me unfairly.
3. When I am under pressure or stressed.
4. Sometimes I may speak loudly or use harsh words when I am angry.

**Question 7: When do you switch into a Passive-Aggressive (sarcasm/gossiping/taunts/silent treatment, etc.) communication style in your day-to-day life?**

1. When I become familiar with a person, so I don't hesitate.
2. When I want to keep my point across but don't want to ignore the other person's point.
3. When someone tries to use me.

**Question 8: How can you make your communication assertive?**

- Speak clearly – Say what I really think or feel.
- Be respectful – Respect other people's opinions even when I disagree.
- Learn to say "No" – I can say no politely when I cannot do something.
- Don't interrupt – Allow others to finish speaking before responding.
- Control my emotions – Take a moment to calm down before speaking when I am angry.
- Avoid blaming – Instead of saying "You always do this," I can explain how the situation affects me.
- Use "I" statements – For example, "I feel uncomfortable when this happens" instead of "You are always wrong."
- Listen to others – Assertive communication is not only about speaking; it also means listening.
- Be confident – Express my opinion without being afraid, but also without forcing it on others.
- Handle problems directly – Instead of using sarcasm, gossip, or silent treatment, I should talk openly about the problem.
